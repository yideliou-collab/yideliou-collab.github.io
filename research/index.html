Research

How do local polar states determine a material's response away from equilibrium? We connect ferroic configurations, collective dynamics and local optical measurements through quantitative physical models.

Polar configurations

Geometry, strain, electrostatic screening, and defects shape ferroic order in complex oxides. We investigate how interfaces, freestanding membranes, and structural disregistry organize ferroelectric and antiferroelectric states, and how these local configurations can be selected or switched.

Collective dynamics

Light perturbs charge, strain, and coupled order parameters on distinct time scales. We ask how the initial domain configuration shapes the resulting trajectory, and use time-resolved observations together with controlled models to distinguish carrier, thermal, elastic, and polarization pathways.

Local manipulation of quantum materials

We investigate how local perturbations change the states and functional responses of quantum materials and complex oxides. Scanning-probe observables are interpreted alongside structural information and quantitative models, keeping measured contrast distinct from inferred polarization, charge, or strain.

A shared modeling framework

Landau–Ginzburg–Devonshire and phase-field models connect free-energy landscapes to evolving textures. Coupled electrostatics, elasticity, and flexoelectricity provide a common framework for interfaces, defects, and nonequilibrium dynamics, implemented with reproducible Python workflows.
